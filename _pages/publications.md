I enjoy doing research that combines academic rigor with practical relevance. My primary research tools are behavioral and experimental methods. My research lies at the intersection of **Behavioral Operations, Socially Responsible Operations, and Decision-Making**, with a particular interest in ethical sourcing, social auditing, supplier responsibility, volunteer management, and how behavioral factors shape managerial decisions.

# **Published Research**

* [**Selection, Payment, and Information Assessment in Social Audits: A Behavioral Experiment**](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/msom.2025.0701) with León Valdés (University of Pittsburgh).
  * **Manufacturing & Service Operations Management**, 2026.
    <!-- <span style="color: orange;">Finalist, POMS College of Service Operations Management (CSOM) Best Student Paper, 2024.</span> -->
  * We use an incentivized laboratory experiment to study how social auditors process information, form beliefs, and make reporting decisions under different auditor-hiring arrangements. We find that auditors are more likely to incorrectly report suppliers as compliant when suppliers have the power to select them. This effect is driven in part by reciprocity: simply being paid by the supplier does not generate the same degree of leniency.

# **Work in Progress**

* **Reference Dependence in Prosocial Tasks: Do Losses Loom Larger Than Gains?** with León Valdés and Mahyar Eftekhar.
  * We examine how mismatches between expected and realized task effort affect people's perceptions of a task and their willingness to engage in it again. Using an incentivized experiment, we study whether these reactions differ when effort benefits oneself versus others and investigate the behavioral mechanisms underlying these responses.

* **Mexican Automotive Industry under Policy Uncertainty: An Experimental Survey** with Arturo Orozco and Francisco Tallabs.
  * We study how executives in the Mexican automotive industry make strategic decisions under policy uncertainty. Using an experimental survey, we examine how different ways of framing the economic and social consequences of policy changes affect managers' evaluations and decisions.

* **Social Mobility and Executive Decision-Making** with Ana Cristina Dahik, Bernardo Sainz, and CEEY.
  * We study how executives' beliefs about social mobility and inequality relate to their support for business initiatives aimed at promoting social mobility. Through an experimental survey, we examine whether framing these initiatives in terms of their social versus business implications affects managers' willingness to support and invest in them.

* **On the Joint Use of Auditing and Training to Motivate Supplier Social Responsibility** with León Valdés.
  * We study how buying firms can combine auditing and supplier training to encourage improvements in supplier social responsibility. Our results suggest that these interventions can have different behavioral consequences: while auditing may increase suppliers' incentives to hide poor conditions, training can reduce hiding and encourage more substantive improvement.

* **Increasing Teachers' Engagement in an Educational App in Rwanda** with León Valdés and Mahyar Eftekhar.
  * We have partnered with a U.S.-based educational nonprofit that develops culturally and linguistically appropriate children's books in settings where access to such materials is limited. Working with teachers in Rwanda, we study how financial and social incentives can increase teachers' engagement with the nonprofit's application and encourage more frequent reading with their students.
